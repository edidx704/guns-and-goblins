## Blessing Descriptions

### Temporary Enchantment (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

This lets the enchanter create magic items that only function a few times before losing their enchantment, at a substantial savings in energy cost (and therefore time).

An item can be temporarily enchanted at an energy cost of 15% the normal cost for an item of that type per use. An item with only a single use can be created for 15% of the normal energy, two uses would cost 30%, four would cost 60% (very cost-ineffective under most circumstances) and the cost for an item limited to 7 or more uses actually exceeds the cost of a permanent item.

The temporarily enchanted item acts, in all respects, as a normal item of that type until all of its uses are consumed. Once all the uses of the temporary item are gone, the item is no longer magical.

Temporary Enchantment can only be used to give items a one-time ability to cast a certain spell — not to cast the spell on the object. For example, a mage couldn't make a one-shot set of invisible armor — it would never wear off. He could give the armor the ability to cast the Invisibility spell on itself at normal energy and duration costs.

**Limitations:** Temporary Enchantment cannot be used in conjunction with any Enchantment college or Meta-Spell college spells except: Hex, Limit, Link, Name, Power, and Speed.

**Duration:** Until all of the item's uses have been expended.

**Cost and time:** See Enchanting, p. 16.

### Fortify (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

Increases the DR of clothing or a suit of armor.

| DR Bonus | Energy Cost |
|----------|-------------|
| +1 | 50 |
| +2 | 200 |
| +3 | 800 |
| +4 | 3,000 |
| +5 | 8,000 |

The spell can be recast to strengthen it, as per Accuracy (p. 65).

**Piece-by-piece pricing:** For individual armor pieces, see the pricing table on p. 66 of *Magic*.

### Hex (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

Makes an item impossible to put down, take off, or get out of. In itself, this effect is merely inconvenient — and can even be useful. A hexed weapon will never be dropped, hexed glasses will never fall off.

However, if the hexed object has another malign spell on it, it becomes much more dangerous. The hex effect is "always on."

To escape from a hexed object, the victim must have the enchantment removed or suppressed, or amputate the body part involved. Attempts to break or cut off the hexed item will fail.

**Limitations:** A hex may be limited by a Name or Password enchantment. For instance, a ring could be made with a Hex and a Strike Blind spell, with the password "Gesundheit." Anyone wearing the ring would be cursed — but should they say the magic word, the ring would fall off!

**Energy cost to create:** 200.

---

### Limit (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

The enchanted item works only for the user, or class of users, specified by the caster when the Limit is cast. This class of users may be as simple or complex as the caster wishes. A Limit spell may affect all the spells on the item, or just some of them.

If an item has multiple spells on it, including one or more limiting spells, the mage casting each new spell determines whether it governs (or is governed by) previous spells. For instance, a named sword might have +3 against orcs for any user, but act as a Dancing Sword only for one who knew its name.

**Energy cost to cast:** 200.

---

### Name (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

Gives a magical name to an enchanted object. The magic of the subject functions only for someone who knows its name. The user must speak the item's name the first time he tries to use it, to show he knows the name.

**Energy cost to cast:** 400, or 200 if the name is written on the item. It must be readable (GM's decision). It may be small, hidden, or even disguised, but a person examining the item must be able to see it. This provision is a major reason for the popularity of dead and obscure languages among wizards — writing your item's name on it in an extinct language is almost as safe as not writing it at all, and much cheaper.

---

### Password (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

The reverse of the Name enchantment. A magical item with a Password functions unless the Password is spoken. If the Password is spoken, the item will be inert until the next time its powers would normally be invoked — or for one minute (choice of person making the item). An item may have some spells that are passworded and others that are not.

**Examples:** (a) a magic ring that gave the power of flight — but puts the wearer in Suspended Animation if he speaks the word "Freeze." (b) A weapon that becomes inert for a minute if the enemy discovers the password and speaks it!

**Energy cost to cast:** 100.

---

### Graceful Weapon (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

This enchantment is a toned-down version of Quick-Draw (below). The item does not become unready after attacking or parrying, even on a critical miss.

If Graceful Weapon encounters Turn Blade or Bladeturning, it resists those spells with its Power.

**Energy cost to cast:** 150 per pound of weight (minimum 1 pound).

**Note:** May be cast on any sort of tool — not just a weapon.

---

### Quick-Draw (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

The weapon will leap to the owner's hand when he wants it, becoming ready instantly (no Ready maneuver required!). Only the owner's mental command is required; no die roll is needed.

The enchantment does not work if the weapon is more than a yard away, or if it is tied down, peacebonded (tied into the scabbard with rope or wire), within a pack, etc. A weapon with this spell does not become unready after attacking or parrying.

**Energy cost to cast:** 300 per pound of weight (minimum 1 pound); 2,000 for a quiver, bag, etc., from which ordinary missiles will leap into the wearer's hand at need.

**Note:** May be cast on any sort of tool — not just a weapon.

---

### Loyal Sword (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

The subject weapon seeks to return to the wearer's hand if it is dropped or thrown — even if the wearer becomes unable to fight.

- **When dropped:** Returns on the next turn, as though Quick-Drawn.
- **When thrown:** Flies until it hits something or falls to the ground, and then returns in as straight a line as possible, with a Move of 12. It avoids enemies and obstacles when returning!
- **If its owner dies:** It falls to the floor — but after that, it becomes loyal to the next person to pick it up.

A loyal weapon can be trapped, captured out of the air, etc., while returning, but it requires a DX-4 roll to catch it, and its constant attempts to return to its owner give anyone else a -4 to fight with it.

A loyal weapon can be sold or given away at any time, but the transaction must be wholly voluntary; it is then loyal to the new owner. It may have only one owner at a time. Limiting spells may be used to limit the class of people who can use it.

**Energy cost to cast:** 750 per pound of the weapon (minimum 1 pound).

**Note:** If this spell is placed on a Dancing Weapon (below) it makes the Dancing Weapon spell almost worthless. The effect will be a Loyal weapon that strikes at foes on its way back to its owner (but at no other time).

### Penetrating Weapon (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

Cutting, impaling, or piercing weapons with this blessing pass through armor as if it were nothing. The weapon gains an armor divisor (p. B268):

| Armor Divisor | Energy Cost |
|---------------|-------------|
| 2 | 250 |
| 3 | 750 |
| 5 | 2,500 |
| 10 | 7,500 |
| Ignores DR | 25,000 |

Such a weapon does not do extra damage to unarmored foes. Hardened DR defends against the armor divisor as normal.

**Cost modifiers:**
- Double the cost if the subject is a missile weapon (e.g., a bow)
- Divide by 10 if the subject is a missile (e.g., an arrow)

If the item falls in more than one class (weapon, missile weapon, missile), use the higher enchantment cost. In addition to weapons, any sort of cutting tool may be enchanted.

**Note:** This spell may be recast at a higher level as per Accuracy.

### Bane (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

This is also considered a Limiting Enchantment. It is used to make a magical weapon with powers that work only against one type of foe.

Successfully cast before any offensive weapon enchantment, it reduces the energy cost of those spells — but they will work only against the named foe. For instance, a sword could be made that did +3 damage, but only against orcs — and this would be easier to enchant than a weapon that did +3 against everything.

**Cost reduction examples:**

| Specificity | Cost Divider |
|-------------|--------------|
| Against a specific nation, religion, or race | ÷2 |
| Against a specific type of creature, or folk of a certain city | ÷3 |
| Against all members of one specific family | ÷4 |
| Against one specific foe | ÷10 |

**Requirements:** The caster must possess some item or relic pertaining to the person, creature, race, etc. involved.

**Energy cost to cast:** 100.

**Note:** A Bane spell cannot be removed unless the enchantments it controls are also removed. The same caster who placed the Bane must cast the enchantments limited by the Bane. Banes like "All my foes" are not allowed!

This spell can be recast to strengthen it, as per Accuracy.

### Lighten (Count 2)

**Enchantment**

Makes armor or shields lighter and easier to carry. The subject becomes lighter only when it is actually being worn. Armor in a backpack would still have its full weight.

**Energy cost to cast:**
- 100 to cut the item's weight by 25%
- 500 to cut its weight in half

### Dancing Weapon (Count 3)

**Enchantment**

A weapon enchanted with this spell fights on its own at the owner's command, hanging in the air as though wielded by an invisible warrior.

**Statistics:** Skill 15, Basic Speed 5, and ST (for damage purposes) equal to its Power.

It uses intelligent strategy and follows its owner's spoken or mental commands about which enemy to attack. It continues fighting until it is dealt with (see below) or its owner dies or tells it to stop.

**Combat rules:**
- Any attack on a dancing weapon is at a penalty: -4 (for a polearm) to -5 (a sword or axe) to -6 (a dagger or pistol)
- If the weapon gets a critical miss, or is struck by a critical hit, it is "stunned" and out of the fight (unless it breaks, in which case it is destroyed)
- If its owner is killed, it falls to the floor — but if he is merely knocked unconscious, it continues to attack the closest foe
- A dancing weapon can be trapped in a net or bag (standard to-hit roll, with bonuses or penalties depending on the cleverness of the method used)

**Autonomous mode:** The weapon can also be left with orders to attack anyone who approaches within a certain distance. A Link can be used to activate it only if a certain event takes place. Once activated, if its owner is not around, it attacks anyone in range — closest first, unless the Link had other instructions.

A dancing weapon cannot change owners while animated; grasping the handle of a dancing weapon is not enough to make it change sides.

**Energy cost to cast:** 1,000 per pound of the weapon's weight (minimum 1 pound). Double the cost to get a weapon with a Basic Speed of 6, or with skill of 18. Multiply cost by 4 to get a weapon with both advantages.

**Limitation:** This spell does not work on a missile weapon!

### Defending Weapon (Count 3)

**Enchantment**

The subject weapon permits its wielder to parry more skillfully; each level of enchantment will give the wielder a +1 to Parry, up to a maximum of +3. The spell does not protect the weapon from being broken (p. B401).

**Combined with Dancing Weapon:** The weapon attacks opponents engaged with the owner and wards off blows from attackers when possible. It uses the weapon's skill as per Dancing Weapon, plus this spell's bonus.

The owner can command it to defend a given side of him (much like Dancing Shield) and to either:
- **Defend:** Gets two parries per turn
- **Attack:** Gets a single attack and a single parry each turn

If a dancing, defending weapon does not parry a blow, the owner may still use any of his own applicable defenses.

**Limitation:** This spell can only be cast on a balanced weapon that can normally parry.

**Energy cost to cast:**
- +1 to Parry: 500
- +2 to Parry: 1,000
- +3 to Parry: 2,000

The spell may be recast at a higher level as per Accuracy.

### Weapon Spirit (Count 3)

**Enchantment (Very Hard)**

Transfers the personality and some of the intelligence of a recently deceased being (humanoid or not — dogs are popular subjects) to a weapon, usually a sword.

**Requirements:**
- Most of the donor's body must be present at the beginning of the enchantment
- The enchantment is at -1 for each full day that elapsed between the donor's death and the beginning of the enchantment
- The body may be disposed of after the enchantment commences

**Transfer options:**
- The enchanter can pass on as little or as much of the donor's IQ as he wishes
- Some of the donor's skills, advantages, and disadvantages may also be transferred
- The weapon has no memory of its former existence
- Quirks and disadvantages sometimes transfer whether the enchanter wants them or not

**Weapon capabilities:**
- Sight and hearing (Per 10), despite no sensory organs
- Communication with wielder through basic empathy ("good feeling" or "bad feeling")
- May be enchanted with Voices to give it speech

**Reaction roll:** Each time it gets a new wielder, the weapon makes one reaction roll that sets the tone for their relationship:
- Very good reaction: Will do anything for its wielder
- Very bad reaction: Refuses to communicate or cooperate

**Energy cost to cast:**
- 100 per point of IQ transferred
- +25 times the character point cost of each skill
- +50 times the total point cost of all the advantages and disadvantages (minimum zero)
- +300 for Voices

**Note:** The weapon's skills cannot be improved, although skills such as History or Area Knowledge may be kept current through the absorption of new knowledge.

### Cornucopia (Count 4)

**Enchantment**

Gives a quiver, bag of pellets, or other container of ammunition an "infinite" supply of one type of ammunition — but items must be taken out by hand, one at a time.

**How it works:**
- Each missile exists for one minute after being removed from the container; then it vanishes
- If picked up by anyone but the Cornucopia's owner, it vanishes immediately
- Therefore, this spell cannot be used to make a single quiver supply enough arrows for an army!
- Cannot be cast on a weapon

**High-tech note:** A high-tech wizard could create a pouch that always contained one armor-piercing tracer shell; he could not enchant a .50-caliber machinegun to fire forever. A medieval mage could not make a "quiver" for high-tech ammo; that ammo is priceless in his world. A Cornucopia quiver can be made to produce magic arrows, but only if the creating mage is capable of making such an arrow — and the energy cost will be tremendous.

**Energy cost to cast:** 50 times the $ value, in the current game world, of the missile provided by the Cornucopia quiver.

**Example:** A quiver that provided ordinary arrows ($2 each) would cost 100 energy to create.

### Adjustable Clothing (Count 5)

**Enchantment**

Causes an item of clothing to adjust to fit whoever wears it, within limits. The adjustment occurs as the wearer begins to put it on, so the enchantment won't be discovered if someone picks up the item and immediately discards it as "obviously" the wrong size.

When removed, the item returns to its original size. The change in size does change weight as well; an object that doubled in size would increase in weight eightfold.

**Uses:**
- Extremely expensive theatrical costumes
- Items intended to outlive their original wearer
- Intended heirlooms

**Energy cost to cast:** The cost to enchant a cloth shirt or pair of pants that can increase or decrease in every dimension by 10% is 50. This cost varies widely depending on size, materials, and adaptability; round fractions up.

| Item Type | Modifier |
|-----------|----------|
| Gloves, boots, shoes, belt, hat, small items | ×½ |
| Ring or earring-sized | ×⅓ |
| Overalls, or a full-length gown | ×2 |
| Pressure suit or other item that covers the whole body | ×3 |
| Thin leather | ×2 |
| Scale or chainmail | ×3 |
| Plate or solid metal | ×5 |
| Veil/lingerie | ×½ |

| Size Range | Modifier |
|------------|----------|
| ±10% | ×1 (base) |
| ±25% | ×2 |
| ×½ to ×2 | ×3 |
| ×⅕ to ×5 | ×4 |

**Enchantment**

The enchanted shield allows its wielder to block more skillfully; each level of enchantment gives the wielder a +1 to Block, up to a maximum of +3.

**Limitation:** May not be combined with Dancing Shield.

**Energy cost to cast:**
- +1 to Block: 500
- +2 to Block: 1,000
- +3 to Block: 2,000

This spell may be recast at a higher level as per Accuracy.

### Quick-Aim (Count 5)

**Enchantment**

The subject missile weapon (bow, sling, etc.) gains uncanny responsiveness; in game terms, an Aim action taken with the subject weapon has the effect of two turns of aiming.

**Energy cost to cast:**
- 100 for one Aim action to count as two turns of aiming
- 200 for one Aim action to count as three turns of aiming

**Modifiers:** Halve the cost if subject is a missile (e.g., a javelin or throwing axe). The enchantment doesn't work on ammunition (like arrows).

**Note:** This spell may be recast at a higher level as per Accuracy.

### Hideaway (Count 6)

**Enchantment**

When cast on an object, Hideaway makes it larger inside than it is outside. Can be used on a pocket, pouch, drawer, pitcher — anything that can normally hold other things.

**Capacity:**
- The caster may add any amount of pound-capacity he can afford
- Increase the size of the "inside" by 2 cubic feet for each pound
- The extra space may be hidden by a false bottom, etc.

**Encumbrance:** Depending on the energy used to cast the spell, objects within the Hideaway may or may not count as encumbrance.

**Duration:** Permanent, but the contents vanish forever if the object is destroyed.

**Finding objects:** The time required to find an object in a Hideaway depends on how deep it is!

**Special:** A large enough Hideaway could hold a person. There is no problem with air unless the Hideaway is sealed off.

**Energy cost to cast:**
- 50 per pound of extra capacity (if contents count as encumbrance)
- 100 per pound (if contents do not count as encumbrance)

### Accuracy (Count 6)

**Enchantment**

Makes a weapon more likely to hit by adding to the user's effective skill.

| Bonus | Energy Cost |
|-------|-------------|
| +1 | 250 |
| +2 | 1,000 |
| +3 | 5,000 |

**Cost modifiers:**
- Divide cost by 10 if the subject is a missile (e.g., an arrow or a bullet)

**Recasting:** If a weapon already has this spell at a low level, a caster may recast the spell at a higher level (making the weapon more accurate). The cost for the new spell is the difference between the levels; the "old" spell does not add to the item's resistance to the "new" one.

### Puissance (Count 6)

**Enchantment**

Adds to the basic damage a weapon does when it hits.

| Damage Bonus | Energy Cost |
|--------------|-------------|
| +1 | 250 |
| +2 | 1,000 |
| +3 | 5,000 |

**Cost modifiers:**
- Divide cost by 10 if the subject is a missile (e.g., an arrow or a bullet)
- Double cost if the subject is a missile weapon (e.g., a bow or a gun)

If the item falls in more than one class (weapon, missile weapon, missile), use the higher enchantment cost.

**Recasting:** May be recast at a higher level, as per Accuracy.

**Houserule: Scaling Puissance (for heavy weapons)**

For weapons with high damage dice, a flat +1 is negligible. Use this rule instead:

> **Puissance adds +1 damage OR +10% of average damage, whichever is better, per level.**

**How to calculate:**
1. Find the weapon's average damage (e.g., 5d+1 = 18.5)
2. Take 10% of that average (e.g., 18.5 × 0.1 = 1.85)
3. Round to the nearest whole number (e.g., +2)
4. Compare to +1 — use whichever is better

**Examples:**

| Weapon       | Damage | Average | +10%      | Bonus | Final     |
| ------------ | ------ | ------- | --------- | ----- | --------- |
| Arrow        | 1d+1   | 4.5     | 0.45 → 0  | +1    | 1d+2      |
| Rifle bullet | 5d+1   | 18.5    | 1.85 → +2 | +2    | 5d+3      |
| Tank shell   | 10d×10 | 350     | +35       | +35   | 10d×10+35 |

*Note: For damage with multipliers (like 10d×10), add the bonus as a flat modifier after rolling, or express as a multiplier increase if the GM prefers (e.g., 10d×11 for +10%).*
